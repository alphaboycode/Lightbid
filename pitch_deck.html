⚠️ Market Pain Points
Web3 Auction Architecture Slide 02 / 11

The Problem: Legacy Web3 Auctions are Broken

Traditional on-chain auctions suffer from predatory MEV bot front-running, contract-level DoS attack vectors, and high friction that destroy true price discovery.

🤖
Last-Second Sniping

Conventional auctions end at a static block timestamp. Automated sniper bots monitor the mempool and broadcast higher bids with exorbitant priority gas in the final 500ms, completely boxing out human bidders.

  • Zero time for legitimate bids to respond
  • Suppresses final clearing prices for creators
  • Enriches MEV searchers at users' expense
🛑
Push-Payment DoS

Standard marketplace contracts automatically "push" refunded funds back to the previous bidder inside the bid() transaction. If a malicious bidder's contract reverts, the entire auction is frozen forever.

  • Reverting receive() breaks bidding loop
  • Attacker locks in low bid as winning bid
  • Reentrancy and state mutation risks
🔄
Wrapping & High Fees

Platforms on Ethereum and L2s force users to wrap native currency into WETH or ERC-20 proxies, generating extra approval transactions, gas burn, and friction for retail collectors and gamers.

  • Multi-step approval & wrap workflows
  • High gas fees ($5–$30 per transaction on L1)
  • Unnecessary cognitive overhead for users
🗂️
Fragile Metadata

Many Web3 auction platforms rely on centralized Web2 AWS S3 buckets or temporary API gateways for media and metadata storage, resulting in dead links, 404 errors, and disappearing artwork.

  • Centralized single point of failure
  • High risk of NFT media "link rot"
  • No integrated creator minting pipeline
